# EEP 2 - The Foundation of Edison

## Abstract

This document says what Edison is, what it is for, and the one rule everything built on it
follows: if it can't be confirmed, it doesn't go in. It also names the three things that rule
protects, and how far any project gets built out. Anyone who builds for Edison, person or
agent, is bound by it.

## Motivation

In the owner's words, most of what he has built was discarded for three reasons: "unneeded
complexity, lack of cohesion, and lack of actually being in accordance to the original
vision."

He also named the cause: "AI kind of hallucinates concepts and I don't know how to confirm or
deny them so they just repeatedly get added to no use." Agents filled the gaps in an unwritten
vision with their own ideas.

This document exists so that an idea nobody can check never gets built on again.

## Specification

### What Edison is

"Edison is both my name and my foundation." Edison is Edison Steele, and it is the foundation
everything he builds stands on. The company, the client work and every repository are things
built on that foundation. None of them is the foundation.

### What Edison is for

A small business gets work built to the standard a big company would get.

### The root rule

**If it can't be confirmed, it doesn't go in. Confirmed means an established standard says
so, or the owner does.**

Confirmation does not rest on the owner alone. In his words: "it shouldn't just be me to
confirm it, it should also be standards as a whole."

Two things count as confirmed:

- a source anyone can open says so; or
- it can be seen working in a real project.

One thing does not count: a second agent agreeing.

Whoever introduces a concept says where it came from. An agent that introduces a concept of
its own labels it as its own, and does not build on it until the owner says so.

### What counts as a standard

In this order. When two disagree, or only some have spoken, the higher one that has spoken is
followed.

1. Law and regulation.
2. Formal standards bodies.
3. The reference publisher, which is Google.
4. Established practice in the wider community.
5. The owner's own call.

When the owner and a standard disagree, the standard wins, unless the owner writes down that
he is departing from it and why. A written, signed departure can be checked. A silent one is
the gap-filling this document exists to stop.

### The three things the rule protects

- **Vision.** Build what the owner wrote down. Where he wrote nothing, ask. Don't fill the gap
  with your own ideas.
- **Simplicity.** Nothing gets added that is of no use to this project.
- **Cohesion.** One idea runs through the whole thing, and every part serves it. Cohesion also
  covers how a thing looks, how its code is structured, and whether it feels like Edison, but
  the one idea comes first.

### How far anything gets built

What every project gets is written once, for all projects, in this series. What one project
gets is written in that project. If it is in neither, it doesn't get built, and the builder
asks.

### What Edison is not

- Not loyal to any one framework.
- Not a different process for every client.
- Not a product for other agencies to buy.

## Rationale

Letting standards confirm, and not only the owner, is what keeps him from being the only
checkpoint. An agent can check an idea against a source without waiting for him. His attention
goes to what no standard covers: taste and vision.

## How to teach this

Tell an agent three things. Build only what is written, in the series or in the project. Say
where every concept came from, and label your own. If you can't confirm it against a source
or a working project, leave it out and ask.

## Reference implementation

The first project built under this document that ships without being discarded.

## Rejected ideas

- **The four rules of the earlier constitution as the foundation** (one home for every fact,
  judgment is signed, nothing until needed, simple first). The owner called them "general and
  mine to some extent". Two of them restate simplicity. None covers cohesion or vision.
- **A second agent's agreement as confirmation.** See the root rule.
- **Edison as only the company.** The company is built on the foundation and is not it.

## Open issues

- **Who comes first when interests collide**, for example when a client wants something that
  hurts the client's own customers. The owner's answer today was "don't know". A real case
  will settle it.
- **The order of vision, simplicity and cohesion when they conflict.** Provisionally vision,
  then simplicity, then cohesion. That order is the drafting agent's and is not confirmed.
- **What an agent does when the owner himself asks for more than a project needs.** The
  drafting agent proposed that it name the cost and ask which project needs it. Not confirmed.
