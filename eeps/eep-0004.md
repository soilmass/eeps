# EEP 4 - Governance of the EEP series

## Abstract

EEP 1 hands the council seven jobs and never says who the council is or what it
may do. This document says. It covers the EEP series and nothing else.

It describes governance as it stands, for a council of one person. Where PEP 13
covers something Edison does not yet need, this document names the event that
will force the answer rather than inventing one now.

## Motivation

Before this document filled the vacancy, EEP 1's substitution table read
`PEP 13 | vacant. Until a governance EEP exists, the owner is the whole council`.
That sentence was doing a great deal of work for a parenthesis.

Through the rest of the table the council accepts and rejects EEPs, appoints
editors, appoints a delegate, grants write access, approves sponsors, judges
what is acceptable, and is the body an issue asks to decide. Nothing states its
composition, its limits, or what happens when it does not act. Two of EEP 1's
open issues wait on this document, and a third, how often the pin is reviewed,
is a council duty if it is anyone's.

The risk is not that the wrong person decides. Today there is only one. The risk
is that the rules get written later, by whoever is in the room, to fit whatever
has just happened.

## Specification

### The council

The council is the owner of Edison, alone.

This is the rule, not a stopgap. A council of one needs no quorum, no vote and
no tie-break, and this document specifies none.

### What the council is for

The council works to:

- protect vision, simplicity and cohesion, as EEP 2 defines them;
- keep the series coherent, so that one idea runs through it and every EEP
  serves that idea;
- decide what is put to it, promptly; and
- act as final appeal, where other means have failed.

The last of these is PEP 13's, whose council is to "act as a 'court of final
appeal' for decisions where all other methods have failed". The council is where
a disagreement stops, not where every question starts.

### What the council decides

- Whether an EEP is accepted, rejected, deferred or withdrawn.
- The status of any EEP, and the resolution recorded against it.
- Who is an editor.
- Whether a delegate is appointed for a given EEP, and who.
- Who has write access to the repository.
- Whether a sponsor who is neither an editor nor a holder of write access is
  approved.
- What counts as acceptable, which EEP 1 already notes PEP 1 leaves deliberately
  circular.

The council should reach for these as little as it can. A standing rule that
settles a class of question is better than deciding each case, and this document
is itself an instance of that preference.

### What the council does not decide

Anything inside a single project. EEP 1 already says such a decision "stays in
that project's own repository and never enters the series", and this document
does not widen that.

The council's authority runs to the EEP series. It is not a charter for the
company, for client work, or for any repository Edison owns.

### How a decision is recorded

Every decision records why. Accept, reject, defer or withdraw: each carries a
reason in the resolution.

PEP 1 requires the `Resolution` header for Standards Track proposals only.
Edison requires the reason for all of them. A decision whose grounds are not
written down cannot be checked later, and EEP 2 asks that of everything else in
the series.

### Changing a decision

The council may reverse itself. A reversal updates the EEP in place: the status
changes and the change history records what changed and why.

PEP 1's `Superseded-By` and `Replaces` headers remain available, and are the
right tools when the reversal is large enough to need a new document. They are
not required for a change of mind about an existing one.

Because EEP 3 makes a change history entry mandatory, a reversal is visible in
the series itself and not only in the repository's history.

### Editors

An editor checks form and never judges. PEP 1 is explicit: "PEP editors don't
pass judgment on PEPs. They merely do the administrative and editorial part".

An agent may be an editor, with no part of the role withheld. A human editor and
an agent editor do the same work under the same rules. EEP 1 already permits
this; this document states that nothing is reserved.

### Sponsors

As EEP 1 says: a person with write access, an editor, or anyone else the council
approves. An agent may be a sponsor.

### Delegates

The council may appoint a delegate to decide one EEP. A delegate decides
finally: in PEP 1's words, the delegate "will then have the authority to approve
or reject that PEP". Appointing one hands over the decision; it does not ask for
a recommendation.

A delegate is never an agent.

No delegate has been appointed.

### Write access

Write access is mechanical. It means a person may merge. It carries no authority
to decide anything and no authority to check form.

This narrows PEP 1, which says that "developers with write access to the PEP
repository may handle the tasks that would normally be taken care of by the PEP
editors". Edison keeps deciding, checking and merging apart, so that who did
which is legible afterwards. PEP 1's wording is permissive rather than required,
so no step of the process is removed.

Nobody but the owner has write access.

### Agents

An agent may be an author, an editor or a sponsor. An agent is never a delegate
and never the council. EEP 1 says this already; it is repeated here because it
is a rule about who decides, and this is the document about who decides.

### Disclosure

When a decision touches the interest of a paying client, the resolution records
that it did. It does not name the client.

There is no recusal. A council of one cannot recuse itself, and pretending
otherwise would be machinery that never runs. What the rule buys is that the
conflict is visible to whoever reads the decision later.

### When the council does not decide

An EEP nobody has decided is marked `Deferred`. PEP 1 provides the status and
the grounds: "The PEP author or an editor can assign the PEP this status when no
progress is being made on the PEP."

`Deferred` is not a rejection and carries no prejudice. It is the honest label
for a proposal waiting on attention it has not had, and it closes EEP 1's open
issue about what happens to proposals when the council has no time for them.

### Reviewing the pin

The council reviews EEP 1's pin of PEP 1 when something prompts it: a defect
found in the pinned text, or a change an EEP needs that the pin does not
support.

There is no fixed interval. EEP 2 asks that nothing be added which nothing
current calls for, and a calendar duty nobody has needed is one more thing to be
in breach of.

### What is not decided yet

Each row below is deferred, not vacant. A vacant step waits on a substitute
being written; these wait on an event. When the event happens, the council
answers the question and this document is updated.

| Not decided | What will force it |
|---|---|
| Who holds final say when more than one person can decide | The first person given write access |
| How council membership changes: terms, removal, succession | A council of more than one |
| How a delegate is chosen, and whether an appointment may be withdrawn | The first EEP the owner does not want to decide himself |
| Whether editors other than the owner are appointed | The first EEP written by someone else |
| Whether the series has topics | The first EEP that does not fit alongside the others |
| Whose interests come first when they collide | A real case. EEP 2 holds this one |

### Changing this document

A change to this document is proposed as a Process EEP or as an update to this
one, and the council decides it. This is the clause EEP 1 uses on itself.

## Rationale

PEP 13 was read and not adopted. It is the right checklist and the wrong
answers: it specifies a five-person committee, elections, terms,
conflict-of-interest recusal, ejection, a vote of no confidence, and a
two-thirds vote of a core team to amend itself. Edison has one person. Adopting
it would have produced a long document almost every clause of which was marked
vacant, which is a document that governs nothing.

So its section list became the questions, and the answers were written fresh.
Composition, mandate, powers, membership, conflicts and amendment are each
answered above because PEP 13 asks each of them, not because they occurred to
anyone.

Specifying only what is needed today is the harder half. The temptation with
governance is to write the constitution for the organisation you hope to have,
which is the failure EEP 1's own motivation describes: every process the owner
wrote for himself "grew elaborate before it was used and was then rebuilt from
the start". The triggers table is the answer to that temptation. It refuses to
invent a rule while still naming the question and the event that will demand it,
so that deferral is a recorded decision rather than an oversight.

## Backwards compatibility

This document changes EEP 1.

The row `PEP 13 | vacant` is no longer vacant and now points here. Two of EEP
1's open issues are resolved: what happens when the council has no time is
answered by `Deferred`, and how often the pin is reviewed is answered above. A
third, who holds final say once other people join, moves here as a trigger
rather than being answered.

Both changes are made in the same pull request as this document, so that EEP 1
never describes a vacancy that has been filled.

Nothing in this document changes how an existing EEP is read. Five decisions
are now recorded, against EEP 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6, and none of them is disturbed.
When this document was drafted there were none.

## How to teach this

Tell someone three things. The council is the owner, and it decides only the EEP
series. Every decision it makes says why. Editors check form, delegates decide
finally, write access only merges, and an agent may be the first two but never
the last two.

## Reference implementation

The first EEP the council decides under this document, with a resolution that
records the reason.

## Rejected ideas

- **Adopting PEP 13 by pin, as EEP 1 adopts PEP 1.** Consistent, and wrong. PEP
  1 is a process that fits a project of any size; PEP 13 specifies an
  institution with a five-person elected committee. Almost every clause would
  have been substituted away to vacant.
- **Specifying full governance now**: composition, terms, elections, removal, no
  confidence. Nothing is waiting on any of it, and EEP 2 forbids adding what is
  of no use to the project today.
- **Giving the council authority over everything Edison owns.** EEP 1 reads PEP
  1's "Python" as every repository Edison owns, client work included, which
  would have supported it. It would also have turned a governance EEP into a
  company charter, and EEP 1 already routes single-project decisions away from
  the series.
- **Recusal for conflicts of interest**, as PEP 13 has. A council of one cannot
  recuse itself. Disclosure is what can actually be performed.
- **Requiring a superseding EEP to reverse a decision.** It keeps a cleaner
  record, at the cost of a new document every time the council changes its mind.
  The change history already makes a reversal visible in the series.
- **A fixed interval for reviewing the pin.** A duty on a calendar that nothing
  has needed is a rule written to be broken.

## Open issues

- **Write access and sponsorship.** This document makes write access purely
  mechanical, but EEP 1's sponsor row still reads a person with write access as
  able to sponsor, following PEP 1. The two are compatible as written, since
  sponsoring is not deciding, but the owner has not been asked whether he
  intended the standing to follow the access.
- **Whether EEP 1's table should record the narrowing.** PEP 1 permits a holder
  of write access to do an editor's work and Edison does not. That is stated
  here; whether it also belongs in EEP 1's substitution table is unsettled.
- **Everything in the triggers table above**, each waiting on its event.
- **Whose interests come first when they collide.** EEP 2 holds this and it
  stays there.
