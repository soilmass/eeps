# EEP 10 - How Edison judges a change

## Abstract

A change reaches Edison as a pull request, and nothing says what makes one
acceptable. EEP 3 gives an editor ten things to check and says plainly that an
editor never judges. EEP 4 says the council decides and that every decision
records why, and never says on what grounds.

This document says. A change passes three gates, in order, and nothing reaches
a gate until the one before it passes.

The first is mechanical: a check decides, and no person looks at what a machine
can refuse. The second is editorial: an agent may hold it, and it reports
findings and never a verdict. The third is the judgment, and it is the only one
that needs the person who decides.

Almost every criterion here is already law, gathered from five documents that
each hold a piece. Two things are not. The four questions in this repository's
`CLAUDE.md` bind nothing where they sit, and EEP 7 says why; they are given
force here. And the list headed "Mine, unconfirmed", which that same file
requires of every pull request, is named as what the third gate reads first,
because it is the judgment's agenda written by whoever wrote the change.

## Motivation

The owner asked for criteria he could apply to a pull request on his own. Five
were open when he asked, and only he may decide them.

What the series gave him was the wrong half.

EEP 3's list is the editor's, and EEP 3 draws its limit from the pinned PEP 1:
"PEP editors don't pass judgment on PEPs. They merely do the administrative and
editorial part." Every item on it is about form.

EEP 4 hands the council seven jobs and a duty to record its reasons. It never
says what a reason may be made of. A council that must record why, and has
nowhere written what counts, records whatever occurred to it that day. That is
the gap-filling EEP 2 exists to stop, arriving at the one place in the series
where no agent can be blamed for it.

Three practical things make it worse.

**Three of EEP 3's ten editor items are checked by nothing.** That the author
has write access or an approved sponsor, that the number is the next available
and below 100 for a meta-EEP, and that a new EEP's status is `Draft`. All three
are mechanical, and every one of them read by a person is attention EEP 2 says
belongs to taste and vision.

**The editor's list covers EEPs only.** Four of the five pull requests open
when this was written were plain changes. EEP 1 says most changes do not need a
proposal, and then says nothing about what makes such a change acceptable. The
kind of change Edison makes most often is the kind least written down.

**The best gate Edison has binds nothing.** This repository's `CLAUDE.md` asks
four questions before anything is added: is it written, does it need a
proposal, can every claim be confirmed, is it of use now. EEP 7 rejected that
location in terms: "Writing this in `CLAUDE.md` instead. It would be read, and
it would bind nothing: EEP 2 says what every project gets is written in this
series, and a file in one repository is not that."

## Specification

### What this binds

Every repository Edison owns, client work included. This is EEP 6's reading and
EEP 7's, and the reasoning is theirs.

**Who holds the third gate is not settled here.** In this series it is the
council, because EEP 4 says so. EEP 4 also says the council's remit "is not a
charter for the company, for client work, or for any repository Edison owns",
so this document does not make the council the judge of a change in a client's
repository. It says a change is judged, and against what. Who judges it is the
repository's own to settle, as EEP 2 leaves to a project what belongs to a
project.

### The three gates

Every change passes three gates, in this order. Nothing reaches a gate until
the one before it has passed, and the order is cheapest rejection first.

1. A check decides.
2. An editor reports.
3. Whoever decides, judges.

The order is the point. A change refused by a check has cost nobody a reading,
and a change that fails on form has cost nobody a judgment.

### The first gate: a check decides

No person looks at a change that a machine can refuse.

Every command the repository has must pass. In this repository that is
`tests/run-fixtures`, `tools/check-eeps`, `tools/check-python`,
`tools/check-adoption` and `tools/build-standard --check`. EEP 7 already
requires every repository to have at least one such command and to wire it to
the host.

This document moves part of EEP 3's unchecked editor items into this gate. Rule
`E018` refuses an EEP whose status is not `Draft` and which carries no
`Resolution`, which is EEP 4's requirement that every decision record its
reason. Rule `R008` refuses a `Process` EEP numbered 100 or above, which EEP 3
says is a meta-EEP and takes a number below 100.

EEP 3's other numbering rule, that an EEP takes "the next available EEP number
not used by a published or in-PR EEP", is not checked and cannot be. It was
written as a rule refusing a gap in the series, and it refused this repository
on a correct state within a minute: EEP 9 was in a pull request, so this tree
skipped 9, and the rule called that a fault. A gap is the normal condition
while a proposal is under review, and a tree cannot see a branch. That half
stays the editor's.

**The third stays the editor's, and cannot be moved.** Whether an author has
write access, or a sponsor the council approved, is a question about who holds
access to the repository. It can be answered only against the host, with
credentials, and this repository is private. A check that asked it would fail
or degrade on every local run. It is named here so that a reader does not take
this gate for more than it is.

### The second gate: an editor reports

An editor checks form and reports what it finds. **An editor never judges**,
which is the pinned PEP 1's rule as EEP 3 states it, and EEP 1 and EEP 4 both
say an agent may be an editor. An editor that concludes a change should be
accepted has stopped being an editor, and its report is worth nothing to the
gate after it.

For an EEP, the list is EEP 3's, less whatever the first gate now decides.

For a plain change the series has had no list at all. Each of these is already
law and names where it comes from:

- The change reached the main branch on a branch, by pull request. *(EEP 7.)*
- Every rule it adds names the source that rule comes from. *(EEP 7.)*
- Every rule it adds has been seen failing. *(EEP 7.)*
- What it takes from outside is pinned at one revision, not copied. Where a
  working copy was unavoidable, it names the revision and every divergence.
  *(EEP 7.)*
- Nothing generated is edited by hand, and whatever refuses a generated file
  that has drifted still passes. *(EEP 7.)*
- Where it adds something Edison did not write, that dependency carries a
  record. *(EEP 9, if the council accepts it. Until then this item is not in
  force and an editor says so rather than applying it.)*

### The third gate: the judgment

In this order, so that the cheapest rejection comes first.

1. **Is it written?** What the change does is in the owner's request, in the
   rules, or in the pinned process. If it is in none of them it does not go in,
   whatever its merits. *(This repository's `CLAUDE.md`, given force here.)*
2. **Did it need a proposal, and not get one?** A change needs one if it binds
   more than one project or changes how the owner works. *(EEP 1, and
   `CLAUDE.md`.)*
3. **Is it of use now?** If nothing current calls for it, it is left out.
   *(EEP 2, simplicity: "Nothing gets added that is of no use to this
   project.")*
4. **Can every claim be confirmed?** Every statement of fact names a source
   anyone can open, with the place in it, or something that can be seen
   working. **A second agent agreeing confirms nothing.** *(EEP 2, the root
   rule.)*
5. **Is it what was written down, and does one idea run through it?** *(EEP 2,
   vision and cohesion.)*
6. **Where it departs from a standard, is the departure written down, with its
   reason?** A written departure can be checked; a silent one is the
   gap-filling EEP 2 exists to stop. *(EEP 2.)*
7. **Record why.** *(EEP 4: "Every decision records why.")*

A change that fails any of the first six is refused or sent back. None of them
is a matter of degree, and none of them is a vote.

### Where the agenda comes from

The third gate is quick or slow depending on one thing, and the series has
never pointed at it.

This repository's `CLAUDE.md` requires every reply and every pull request to
end with a list headed "Mine, unconfirmed", naming each idea that came from
neither the owner nor a source. That list is criterion 4 already compiled, by
whoever wrote the change, before it was asked for.

**It is read first.** Everything on it is unconfirmed by definition, so it is
the whole of what the third gate has to decide about the change's claims. If
everything on it is acceptable, criteria 3 to 6 are largely discharged; if
anything on it is not, the change goes back without the rest being read.

Every repository Edison owns requires that list of a pull request. Like the
four questions above, it lives today in one repository's instructions and binds
nothing, and this is where it comes into force.

### What a decision is

An EEP is accepted, rejected, deferred or withdrawn, which are the pinned
PEP 1's four as EEP 4 names them.

A plain change is merged, sent back for changes, or closed. EEP 4 did not name
these because it governs the series and not a repository's ordinary work.

Both carry the reason, which is EEP 4's rule applied to the kind of change it
did not mention.

### What is not settled here

What any particular change should contain. This document says how a change is
judged, not what to build.

Who holds the third gate in a repository that is not this series.

Whether an agent may hold the second gate in client work, where a client may
have a view about who reads their code.

## Rationale

**This is mostly a gathering, and says where it is not.** EEP 8's reasoning
applies: a rule written in five places drifts between its copies, and EEP 1's
`PEP 12` row stayed wrong for four commits because nothing checked it. Every
criterion above names the document it comes from, so a reader can check the
gathering rather than trust it.

Two things are not gathered. The four questions and the "Mine, unconfirmed"
list are practice in one repository and law nowhere, and EEP 7 says exactly why
that is not enough. Giving them force is a change and is marked as one, here
and in the open issues, rather than carried in under the word "gathering".

**Why three gates and not one list.** A single list makes the owner read
everything to find the part only he can answer. Splitting them by who can
answer is what lets the first two be held by a machine and an agent, which is
what EEP 2 asks for when it says his attention goes to what no standard covers.
The order matters for the same reason: a change refused by a check has cost
nobody a reading.

**Why an editor may be an agent and may not judge.** Both halves are already
decided. EEP 1's substitution table says "PEP editors | editors appointed by
the council. An agent may be an editor", and EEP 4 says an agent is never the
council. EEP 3 carries PEP 1's own limit. This document adds nothing to that
and only puts the editor in a place in the order.

**Why the criteria are not a score.** EEP 2's root rule is a gate and not a
weighting: "If it can't be confirmed, it doesn't go in." A change that fails
criterion 4 is not offset by being useful. Nothing here is a matter of degree,
and there is no tie to break, which is also why a council of one needs no vote.

**Why the type is `Process`.** The pinned PEP 1 decides it: a Process PEP
covers "procedures, guidelines, changes to the decision-making process", and
unlike an Informational PEP is "more than recommendations", so that "users are
typically not free to ignore them". This is a change to the decision-making
process and must not be ignorable.

## How to teach this

Tell a builder: your change passes three gates. The checks must pass, an editor
checks its form and only its form, and then whoever decides judges it. Tell
whoever decides: read the "Mine, unconfirmed" list first, because it is the
list of things nobody has confirmed yet, and that is what you are for. Then ask
the seven questions in order and stop at the first one that fails.

## Reference implementation

The first pull request judged by this document and recorded.

Five were open when it was written and none had been judged by it, which is
stated in the open issues rather than glossed. As a Process EEP this is likely
to settle at `Active`, as EEP 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 did, since a rule of
decision is never meant to be completed.

## Rejected ideas

- **One list instead of three gates.** Simpler to write and it makes the owner
  read every item to find the ones only he can answer. The split is by who can
  answer, which is what lets two thirds of it be held by a machine and an
  agent.
- **Letting an editor recommend.** It would save the third gate a reading and
  it destroys the thing that makes an editor useful. The pinned PEP 1 draws the
  line and EEP 3 carries it: an editor does "the administrative and editorial
  part" and nothing else.
- **Scoring a change, or weighing the criteria against each other.** EEP 2's
  root rule is a gate. A change that cannot be confirmed is not redeemed by
  being useful, and inventing a weighting would be a rule nobody decided.
- **Leaving the four questions in `CLAUDE.md`.** They are well stated and they
  bind nothing where they are, for the reason EEP 7 gives about itself. A
  second repository would not get them, and whoever built it would use whatever
  he was used to.
- **Writing a check for the author's write access.** It is the one unchecked
  editor item that stays unchecked. It needs the host, credentials and a
  repository that is not private, and a check that degrades on every local run
  teaches a reader to ignore it.
- **Making this bind only the series.** Narrower and safer against EEP 4. It
  would also mean the second repository settles all of this again, which is the
  failure EEP 7 was written to stop. The remit problem is answered instead by
  leaving who holds the third gate to the repository.

## Open issues

- **Nothing has been judged by this.** Five pull requests were open when it was
  written and this document generalises no instance at all. EEP 3 asks that
  this section be emptied before a decision is asked for; it is not, as it was
  not for EEP 1, 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8.
- **Two obligations come into force here that were practice in one
  repository.** The four questions and the "Mine, unconfirmed" list now bind
  every repository Edison owns. Whether the second is proportionate for a small
  client repository has not been asked.
- **EEP 3's editor item 1 is checked by nothing and cannot be checked here.**
  Whether it should be checked once the repository is public, or dropped, is
  undecided.
- **`R008` decides less than EEP 3 asks, and the rest cannot be checked.**
  EEP 3 says the number is the next available "not used by a published or in-PR
  EEP". A tree cannot see an EEP in a pull request on another branch, so what
  was written first, a rule refusing a gap in the series, refused a correct
  repository. Only the meta-EEP bound is checked. Whether the whole rule could
  be checked against the host, once the repository allows it, is undecided.
- **Whether the seven criteria are in the right order.** They are ordered so
  the cheapest rejection comes first, which is a guess about which rejections
  are common. Nothing has tested it.
- **Whether a plain change needs its reason recorded anywhere durable.** EEP 4
  requires a resolution of an EEP. A merged pull request carries its reason in
  a thread, and nothing says that is enough.
