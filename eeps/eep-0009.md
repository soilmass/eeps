# EEP 9 - How Edison decides what to depend on

## Abstract

Edison depends on things it did not write: libraries, frameworks, the tools it
runs, the services it pays for. Nothing in the series says how one is chosen.
EEP 8 says how Edison adopts an outside **text**, and says nothing about a
thing that runs or is paid for.

This document settles the decision. Before Edison depends on anything, four
steps run in order. Can it be avoided by something already depended on? Does
the candidate clear a bar? If it does, Edison uses it rather than writing its
own, and writing its own instead is a departure only the council may sign. Does
it serve the one idea the project is built around?

The bar itself is **vacant**. EEP 2 puts the owner's own call last among the
things that may confirm something, usable only where nothing higher has spoken,
and on this question something higher has spoken. Writing the bar here would
invert the ranking the series runs on. It is left to be filled by adoption,
which is what EEP 8 exists for.

What this document does supply now is the record. Every dependency carries one,
with a field for each of the four steps, so that a decision nobody wrote down
cannot pass for one that was made.

## Motivation

The owner asked for the series to be built out until tooling could enforce what
Edison has adopted, and said that he would rather buy than build wherever the
options meet certain criteria.

The criteria were not written anywhere, and neither was the preference.

EEP 8 looks like the place they would live and is not. It governs an adoption:
a text published outside Edison, pinned at one revision and read through a
table of substitutions. Google's Python style guide is such a text. The linter
that checks it is not. EEP 8 has no form for a library, a command, a hosted
service or an invoice, and those are most of what Edison will depend on.

EEP 2 does not fill the gap either. Its ranking says whose word settles a
question of fact. It does not say whether to run an existing checker or write
one.

The cost of the silence is already visible in this repository. Rules `M001` to
`M009` in `tools/check-eeps` are generic Markdown and prose checks, written by
hand in Python, in a repository whose EEP 7 says that "what is taken from
outside is pinned, not copied". Nobody decided to write them rather than take
something; there was nothing to decide against. Whether they survive this
document is left open below, and it is the first real test of it.

The failure EEP 2 exists to stop arrives here the same way EEP 7 found it
arriving: not as an agent inventing a rule, but as nobody having written one,
so that whoever is in the room supplies it from habit.

## Specification

### What this binds

Every repository Edison owns, client work included.

This is EEP 6's reading and EEP 7's, and the reasoning is theirs. The council
decides this document; this document binds the repositories. EEP 4 sets the
council's remit at the series and says it is "not a charter for the company,
for client work, or for any repository Edison owns". That is untouched.

### What a dependency is

Anything Edison relies on that Edison did not write: a library, a framework, a
tool it runs, a service it pays for, a platform it builds on.

This draws a line the series has not drawn. **EEP 8 governs texts that bind.
This document governs things that run or are paid for.** Google's Python style
guide is an adoption under EEP 8. `pylint` is a dependency under this document.
One repository file is arguably both, and that is recorded as an open issue
rather than settled here.

Only **direct** dependencies are decided. A transitive dependency arrives with
its parent and gets no decision of its own. EEP 2's second protected thing is
simplicity, and a rule that demanded a decision for every package in a
dependency tree would not be used.

### The order of decision

Before Edison depends on anything, in this order.

**1. Necessity.** Can it be avoided by something the repository already depends
on? If it can, it is not added.

The source is the OpenSSF Concise Guide for Evaluating Open Source Software,
whose first rule is "Consider Necessity": "Evaluate whether the dependency can
be avoided by utilizing existing components. Every new dependency increases the
attack surface (a subversion of the new dependency, or its transitive
dependencies, may subvert the system)". That is established practice in the
wider community, which EEP 2 ranks fourth.

**2. The gate.** Does the candidate clear the bar?

Vacant. See the next section.

**3. The default.** If the candidate clears the gate, Edison uses it. Writing
Edison's own instead is a **departure from this document**, and only the
council may sign one.

The source is the Federal Acquisition Regulation, 48 CFR 12.101, which binds
the head of a federal agency to "Acquire commercial products, commercial
services, or nondevelopmental items when they are available to meet the needs
of the agency". That is law and regulation, which EEP 2 ranks first. It binds
federal agencies and not Edison, and it is **cited here and not adopted**: what
is taken from it is that the highest tier of Edison's own ranking, where it has
spoken on this question at all, says acquire.

The regulation states a soft preference. The hardness, the council's signature,
is the owner's own call under EEP 2's fifth rank, where nothing higher has
spoken, and the council-only signature follows EEP 7, which holds that only the
council may excuse a repository from an EEP.

Writing Edison's own because **no** candidate clears the gate is not a
departure. It is this document working, and it needs no signature. Only writing
Edison's own in the face of a candidate that clears the gate is a departure.

**4. Fit.** Does it serve the one idea the project is built around?

EEP 2 already supplies this, as vision, simplicity and cohesion. Nothing is
invented for it here.

### The gate is vacant

Vacant in the sense EEP 8 gives the word: it stays in the process, it is
skipped for now, and it is filled as soon as it can be.

While it is vacant, steps 1, 3 and 4 run unchanged and **eligibility rests on
the judgment of whoever is building**, which the record has to state. It is not
the council's judgment. EEP 4 puts "anything inside a single project" outside
what the council decides, and a council ruling on every dependency in every
repository would be the charter EEP 4 disclaims. What the council may do, and
what step 3 asks of it, is sign a departure from an EEP.

**This is the weakest part of this document and it is not disguised.** A
builder judging a candidate against nothing written is the gap-filling EEP 2
exists to stop. It is accepted here for one reason: the alternative is to write
the bar at EEP 2's fifth rank when its fourth has spoken, which inverts the
ranking the series runs on. It makes the adoptions below urgent rather than
optional, and it is recorded in the open issues.

Filling it needs two adoptions, not one, because no single text found covers
both what Edison takes from open source and what it buys:

- For open-source dependencies, the OpenSSF Open Source Project Security
  Baseline, in `github.com/ossf/security-baseline` under Apache-2.0. It states
  sixty-seven requirements in the imperative, in machine-readable form, each
  marked with the maturity at which it applies.
- For paid services and closed-source products, Minimum Viable Secure Product,
  in `github.com/vendorsec/mvsp` under CC0-1.0.

Neither is adopted here, and the council has adopted neither. They are named so
that the next author has somewhere to start and so that the shape of the
vacancy is visible. Adopting either is an EEP written under EEP 8, and EEP 8
says an adoption EEP makes one adoption, so there are two of them.

### The recorded decision

Every direct dependency carries a record, in the repository that takes it on,
with one field for each step, so that no step is taken without evidence:

1. What it is, what it is needed for, and why it could not be avoided by
   something the repository already depends on.
2. What was considered, and the result at the gate; or, while the gate is
   vacant, the builder's judgment and its grounds.
3. The decision: used; or written by Edison instead, with the council's
   signature and the reason.
4. How it serves the one idea.

This document does not say which file the record lives in. EEP 7 already
requires a README that says where things live, and EEP 2 leaves to the project
what the project decides.

### How far a check can decide this

A check can compare the dependencies a repository declares against the record
and refuse a direct dependency that has none; refuse a record missing any of
the four fields; and refuse a third field that says Edison wrote its own while
carrying no signature.

Fields one, two and four are prose. A check can establish that they are present
and not that they are true. EEP 7 requires a check that cannot decide to say
so, and this is the boundary: what is enforced here is that a decision was
recorded, not that it was a good one.

### What is not settled here

What the gate holds, and at what level. Both are for the adoptions.

Anything about transitive dependencies beyond that they arrive with their
parent.

What a repository is for, or what it may be built out of. This document says
how a dependency is decided. It names no language, no framework and no vendor.

## Rationale

**The ranking did the work.** EEP 2 says that when two sources disagree, "the
higher one that has spoken is followed". Rank one has spoken on the default and
rank four on necessity. The two do not in fact conflict: the regulation speaks
to how Edison obtains a thing it needs, and the guide to whether it needs one.
Read in that order neither is departed from, and no signed departure is owed.
The order is the drafting agent's and is named as such in the acknowledgements.

**Why the gate is vacant rather than written.** It would have been quicker to
write a bar here and enforce it tomorrow. EEP 2 puts the owner's own call fifth
and usable where nothing higher has spoken; on this question rank four has
spoken, in a document that is free to read, pinnable and already machine
readable. Writing a bar at rank five in front of it would be the owner
overruling a standard without the written departure EEP 2 requires, in the one
document whose whole subject is preferring what exists to what one writes
oneself. The vacancy is the honest cost of taking the series at its word.

**Why the council signs a departure but does not judge eligibility.** These
look alike and are not. EEP 4 reserves the council's authority to the series
and disclaims any charter over a repository; judging every dependency would be
that charter. Signing a departure from an EEP is what EEP 7 already establishes
the council may do, and it is the same act. Drawing step 3 as a departure keeps
the hard default from widening the council's remit by accident.

**Why a record, and why one field per step.** A step whose result is not
written down cannot be checked afterwards, and EEP 6's motivation states the
consequence: "a standard nobody wrote down is a standard nobody can be held
to". One field per step is what makes the record evidence rather than a note.
It is also the only part of this document a tool can act on while the gate is
vacant, which is what the owner asked the series to be built out for.

**Why only direct dependencies.** A decision for every package in a tree would
be unusable and would be abandoned, and an abandoned rule is worse than none.
The parent's decision covers what it brings.

**Why this cites and does not adopt.** EEP 8: "Adopting is not citing. An EEP
may quote or cite an outside document without adopting it". Neither source here
is adopted. Two of the reasons are practical, and one is not. The Federal
Acquisition Regulation as published by its issuing agency carries no licence
file, so its terms cannot be read from the repository, and EEP 8 requires an
adoption to record the licence of the text it adopts. The OpenSSF guide is a
guide to evaluating candidates, so what it would fill is the gate, and the gate
is being filled deliberately and by an adoption EEP of its own. The reason that
is not practical is that neither text governs how Edison works. Each supplies
one sentence of reasoning. That is a citation, and calling it an adoption would
cheapen a word this series depends on.

**Why the type is `Process`.** The pinned PEP 1 decides it. A Process PEP
covers "procedures, guidelines, changes to the decision-making process, and
changes to the tools or environment used in Python development" and, unlike an
Informational PEP, is "more than recommendations", so that "users are typically
not free to ignore them". A rule about what a repository may depend on is a
guideline about the environment work is done in, and it must not be ignorable.
EEP 6 and EEP 7, the other two that bind repositories, are `Process` for the
same reason.

## Backwards compatibility

Every repository Edison owns already holds dependencies chosen before this
document existed. None of them has a record.

This document binds a dependency taken on after the council accepts it. An
existing dependency gets a record the next time it is changed, or the next time
the repository is worked on in a way that touches it.

A sweep of everything at once was considered and is not proposed. EEP 1's
motivation names the failure it would be: a process that "grew elaborate before
it was used". The proposal in this section is the drafting agent's and the
council decides it.

## Security implications

The gate is a security gate, and it is vacant.

Until the two adoptions named above are accepted, nothing in the series states
a security bar that a dependency must clear, and the judgment of whoever is
building is the whole of it. A reader who sees four steps may take this
document to be stronger than it is. It requires that a dependency be recorded.
It does not yet require that it be safe.

## How to teach this

Tell a builder: before you add anything Edison did not write, ask whether you
can avoid it. If you cannot, judge whether the candidate is good enough. That
judgment is yours for now, because the bar is not written yet. If it is good
enough, use it, because writing your own instead needs the owner's signature.
Then write down, in four parts, what you decided and why.

## Reference implementation

The first dependency decided under this document and recorded.

Under EEP 1 a reference implementation is "a real project that used the
proposal", and an EEP may not become `Final` until that project has shipped. It
may be accepted before then. As a Process EEP this is likely to settle at
`Active`, as EEP 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 did, since a rule of decision is never
meant to be completed.

## Rejected ideas

- **Writing the gate here, at the owner's own rank.** The fastest route to
  something enforceable, and the one this document exists instead of. EEP 2
  ranks the owner's call fifth and a published practice fourth, and rank four
  has spoken. See the rationale.
- **Letting the council judge eligibility while the gate is vacant.** The
  obvious way to keep the default working, and it is closed. EEP 4 puts
  "anything inside a single project" outside what the council decides and says
  its remit "is not a charter for the company, for client work, or for any
  repository Edison owns". A council that ruled on every dependency in every
  repository would be that charter.
- **One document covering the decision and both adoptions.** EEP 8 says an
  adoption EEP makes one adoption. Three subjects in one document would also be
  three arguments the council has to accept or reject together.
- **Adopting the standards published by the formal standards bodies.** They are
  ranked second and were surveyed first. Their texts are sold rather than
  published, and none of them is one file in a git repository. EEP 8 already
  decides what follows: "Where the adopted text does not live in a git
  repository, there is no pin in this form and the adoption cannot be written
  under this document." That closes the rank as a class, not one standard at a
  time, and recording it once here is cheaper than re-deciding it at every
  adoption. What their licence terms say was not established, because the
  publisher's site refused every request made to it; see the open issues.
- **Adopting the reuse practice in the Secure Software Development Framework.**
  Directly on the question, free to read, and published by a body ranked
  second. It is in no git repository, so it cannot be pinned under EEP 8. Every
  public repository of its publisher was listed and searched before this was
  written; that publisher does put four of its other publications on the same
  host, which makes the absence worth recording rather than assuming.
- **Adopting the dependency-management chapter of Google's book on software
  engineering.** The closest thing found to the gate, from the publisher EEP 2
  ranks third. Its authors disclaim it in its own text, calling it "this
  (incomplete) list of questions" and adding "we can't give a perfect formula
  for deciding when it's cheaper in the long term to import versus reimplement;
  we fail at this ourselves, more often than not". It is also published under a
  licence whose no-derivatives term forbids the substitution table an adoption
  is read through, and whose non-commercial term does not reach client work.
- **Adopting the two OpenChain specifications.** Pinnable, permissively
  licensed, and pointed the wrong way. Every requirement in them binds the
  adopting organisation as a **supplier** of software: "supplied software" is
  defined as "software that an organization distributes to third parties". This
  document is about what Edison takes in. Both also confine themselves to open
  source, so neither reaches what Edison buys.
- **Adopting the federal source code policy's three-step analysis.** The
  closest published procedure for choosing between buying and building. It
  requires that solutions be considered "equally and on a level playing field,
  and free of preconceived preferences based on how the technology is
  developed, licensed, or distributed", which is exactly the preference EEP 8
  states when it requires an adopted text to live in a public repository.
  Adopting it would put the series in contradiction with itself.
- **A list of approved dependencies, maintained centrally.** It would answer
  the question faster than four steps. It also goes stale silently, it is the
  copy-rather-than-pin failure EEP 7 already forbids, and it decides for a
  project what EEP 2 leaves to the project.

## Open issues

- **The gate is vacant**, and until it is filled eligibility rests on the
  builder's judgment against nothing written. That is knowingly the
  gap-filling EEP 2 exists to stop, accepted for the reason the rationale
  gives. EEP 3 asks that this section be emptied before a decision is asked
  for; it is not, as it was not for EEP 1, 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8.
- **EEP 8 pins one file, and one of the two texts named above is eight
  files.** EEP 8's table gives `### The pin` as holding "the pin sentence",
  which an editor might read as permitting one sentence or several. Until that
  is settled, the first of the two adoptions cannot be written.
- **No dependency has ever been decided under this.** The document is written
  before its first use, deliberately, on the reasoning EEP 5 and EEP 7 both
  used: leaving it to the week it is first needed is the worst time to write
  it. That is still one instance short of evidence.
- **Whether a record for every direct dependency stays proportionate.** EEP 7
  carries the same doubt about its own obligation to have a check, and nothing
  scales either to the size of the work.
- **Whether rules `M001` to `M009` in `tools/check-eeps` survive step 3.** Nine
  generic Markdown and prose rules, written by hand, in a repository that would
  now need the council's signature to write its own where a candidate clears
  the gate. This document does not answer it. It is the first real test of the
  document and it is named so that it is not quietly skipped.
- **Whether `tools/pylintrc` is an adoption, a dependency, or both.** It is a
  working copy of an adopted text that is also configuration for a tool Edison
  runs. The line drawn above does not cleanly cut it.
- **Whether the two named texts are the right two.** They survived a survey and
  the survey was not exhaustive. An adoption EEP has to show its own work under
  EEP 8, and naming them here does not do that work in advance.
- **The terms on which the rank-two standards are published were not read.**
  Every request to the publisher's site was refused with an HTTP 403, so what
  its texts say about reproduction is not confirmed and is not asserted above.
  The ground given for rejecting the rank is the one that could be confirmed:
  those texts are not in a git repository, so EEP 8 has nothing to pin. If the
  terms are ever read and permit a copy, the rejection should be revisited.
